        We are always interested in hearing from talented and curious people
        who want to push the boundaries of what theoretical and computational
        chemistry can do. The group works at the intersection of physical
        chemistry, applied mathematics, condensed matter physics, quantum
        information science, and biophysics, so we welcome people whose
        backgrounds span any of these areas — or who simply want to grow into
        them.

        Our research thrives on a mix of analytical theory, careful numerics,
        and close engagement with experiment. We expect group members to take
        ownership of their projects, to think rigorously about both the
        physics and the methods, and to communicate their work clearly to
        chemists, physicists, and computer scientists alike. In return, we
        offer a collaborative environment with substantial mentoring,
        access to advanced computational resources, and active collaborations
        with leading experimental groups around the world.

      text: |
        Prospective PhD students should apply directly to one of the graduate
        programs at CU Boulder:

        * [Department of Chemistry](https://www.colorado.edu/chemistry/graduate-program)
        * [Department of Physics](https://www.colorado.edu/physics/graduate-program)

        After admission, students informally rotate through groups before formally
        joining a research advisor. If you are interested in our work, we
        strongly encourage you to:

        1. Read one or two of our recent papers and think about which
           directions excite you most.
        2. Mention your interest in theoretical chemistry and our group in
           your application materials.
        3. Email Andrés ([andres.montoyacastillo@colorado.edu](mailto:andres.montoyacastillo@colorado.edu))
           with a short note about your background, what drew you to our
           research, and any questions you have.

        We welcome students from chemistry, physics, applied math, materials
        science, and computer science backgrounds. A foundation in
        quantum mechanics, statistical mechanics, and some
        programming experience (Python, C/C++, Fortran, or Julia) will help
        you hit the ground running, but enthusiasm and willingness to learn
        matter most.

        We periodically have postdoctoral openings supported by group funding,
        and we are always open to hosting candidates with their own
        fellowships.

        If you are interested in joining as a postdoc, please email Andrés
        directly with:

        * A short cover letter describing your scientific interests and how
          they align with the group.
        * Your CV, including a list of publications.
        * A 1–2 page research statement outlining what you would like to work
          on.
        * The names and contact information of three references.

        We are especially happy to support applications for external
        fellowships, including: 

        * [Schmidt Science Fellows](https://schmidtsciencefellows.org/)
        * [Chancellor’s Postdoctoral Fellowship](https://www.colorado.edu/postdoctoralaffairs/current-postdocs/chancellors-postdoctoral-fellowship)

        We encourage CU Boulder undergraduates interested in theoretical chemistry
        research to reach out. We have mentored undergraduates on
        projects ranging from computational spectroscopy to biophysics, and we are committed to giving undergraduates a real research experience — not just a side task. We find that it is helpful to have had exposure to coding (e.g., Python or Julia), thermodynamics and/or statistical mechanics, and quantum mechanics. 

        We also welcome inquiries from students who are interested in participating in formal research programs, like CU Boulder's [Undergraduate Research Opportunities Program (UROP)](https://www.colorado.edu/urop/) and the Chemistry Department's Toji Fellowship program.

        All graduate students in the group are fully supported through a
        combination of teaching assistantships, research assistantships, and
        fellowships. Postdoctoral researchers are supported through group

        which provide intellectual independence and broaden your research
        opportunities. Programs to consider include:

        * [DOE Computational Science Graduate Fellowship (CSGF)](https://www.krellinst.org/csgf/)
        * [DOE Office of Science Graduate Student Research (SCGSR)](https://science.osti.gov/wdts/scgsr)
        * [Hertz Foundation Fellowship](https://www.hertzfoundation.org/)
        * [NDSEG Fellowship](https://www.ndsegfellowships.org/)

        The group is currently supported by the U.S. Department of Energy
        Early Career Award, the Packard Fellowship for Science and
