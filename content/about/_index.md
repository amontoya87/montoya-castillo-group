        [Andrés Montoya-Castillo](https://www.colorado.edu/chemistry/andres-montoya-castillo)
        is an Assistant Professor of Chemistry at the University of Colorado
        Boulder. He is also part of the
        [Materials Science and Engineering (MSE) Program](https://www.colorado.edu/mse/)
        and the
        [Interdepartmental Biophysics Program](https://www.colorado.edu/biophysics/).
        His research centers around developing and applying theory
        and simulation methods to investigate the dynamics of chemical
        processes in the condensed phase. Andrés has received recognitions
        for his work, including the
        [Department of Energy Early Career Award](https://www.colorado.edu/asmagazine/2023/09/27/molecule-movement-coastal-flooding-cu-scientists-push-boundaries)
        (2023), an NSF CAREER Award (2025), and an ACS OpenEye/Cadence
        Outstanding Junior Faculty Award (2026). He has been selected as a
        [Packard Fellow](https://www.packard.org/insights/news/the-david-and-lucile-packard-foundation-announces-the-2024-class-of-packard-fellows-for-science-and-engineering/)
        (2024), a
        [Scialog Fellow for Quantum Matter and Information](https://rescorp.org/scialog/quantum-matter-and-information/)
        (2025), a [Sloan Research Fellow](https://sloan.org/fellowships/2026-Fellows) (2026), and a
        [Camille Dreyfus Teacher–Scholar](https://www.dreyfus.org/2026-camille-dreyfus-teacher-scholar-awards/)
        (2026). Andrés serves on the Early Career Editorial Advisory Board
        for the [Journal of Chemical Physics](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/jcp)
        and on the Editorial Advisory Board of
        [APL Computational Physics](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/aco).

        Andrés obtained his Ph.D. in Chemical Physics (2011–2016) from
        Columbia University working with
        [David Reichman](http://www.columbia.edu/cu/chemistry/groups/reichman/index.html) and did his postdoc in the [Markland group](https://web.stanford.edu/group/markland/index.html) at Stanford University (2016-2020). 
        

        Andrés is deeply committed to teaching, mentoring, promoting equity,
        and fostering diversity. He received the
        [Marinus Smith Award](https://www.colorado.edu/orientation/marinus-smith-awards)
        (2024), which "recognizes faculty and staff members who have had a
        particularly positive impact on [CU Boulder] students." Andrés's
        teaching has also been recognized by the
        [Jack Miller Award for excellence in teaching](https://chem.columbia.edu/department-life/student-awards/jack-miller-teaching-awards/)
        while he was at Columbia University.

# on developing methods to simulate quantum dynamics involving
# multiple electronic states coupled to nuclear motions, i.e.,
# nonadiabatic dynamics, that encode the mechanisms of energy and
# charge transfer in the condensed phase. As a postdoc in the
# [Markland group](https://web.stanford.edu/group/markland/index.html)
# at Stanford University (2016–2020), Andrés worked on a wide variety
# of problems, ranging from developing an exact Cartesian map for
# fermionic operators to address the structure and dynamics of
# many-electron problems, to developing new methods and systematically
# assessing existing approaches to calculate the optical spectroscopy
# of chromophores in solution, and constructing an accurate and
# efficient DFT diabatization method to set the stage for the
# on-the-fly *ab initio* treatment of nonadiabatic dynamics in the
# condensed phase.
# Over the past decade, Andrés
# has mentored more than 20 high school, undergraduate, and graduate
# students through the Markland group at Stanford,
# [oSTEM](https://www.ostem.org/), the
