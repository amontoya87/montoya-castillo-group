Andrés Montoya-Castillo is an Assistant Professor of Chemistry at the University of Colorado Boulder. He received his PhD in Chemical Physics from Columbia University in 2016 under the supervision of David Reichman, and completed his postdoctoral training at Stanford University working with Tom Markland before joining CU Boulder.

The AMC Group works at the interface of physical chemistry, applied mathematics, condensed matter physics, quantum information science, and biophysics. The group develops and applies a unified toolkit spanning dimensionality reduction techniques, Mori-Zwanzig theory, and field theories alongside numerical methods including molecular dynamics, electronic structure theory, enhanced sampling, and machine learning — all aimed at the same fundamental goal: making previously intractable simulations tractable.

Our advances lower the cost of challenging condensed-phase simulations by multiple orders of magnitude, bridging static, dynamic, and statistical descriptions of chemical systems to uncover and manipulate the mechanisms of energy, charge, and information flow in complex materials.
