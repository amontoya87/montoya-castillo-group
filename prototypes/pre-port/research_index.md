      subtitle: >
        We develop and apply theoretical and simulation methods to investigate
        the dynamics of condensed phase systems, straddling the boundaries
        between physical chemistry, condensed matter physics, and quantum
        information science.
      text: |
        Our methods exploit the hierarchy of time- and length-scales inherent
        in condensed phase processes to illuminate the wealth of data from
        cutting-edge experiments — experiments that now offer unparalleled
        time- and energy-resolution, providing an extraordinary opportunity to
        vet and advance theory. We aim to deliver physically transparent models
        that enable us to understand and manipulate the optical, electronic, and
        quantum properties of complex materials.

      title: "Biophysical Transformations Across Timescales"
      subtitle: "Molecular mechanisms of disease & therapeutic design"
      text: |
        Many diseases — including neurodegenerative conditions — are governed
        by slow, subtle conformational changes in large biomolecules that are
        invisible to standard simulation approaches and difficult to resolve
        experimentally. These rare but consequential transitions control
        biological function and dysfunction alike.

        We develop theoretical and computational methods to access these slow
        timescales, uncovering the molecular mechanisms that underlie disease
        progression and revealing the design principles needed for rational
        development of molecular treatments. By bridging accurate quantum
        dynamics with the statistical sampling required in the condensed phase,
        we connect fundamental physics to therapeutic insight.

      title: "Out-of-Equilibrium Energy Flow in Complex Materials"
      subtitle: "Spectroscopy, microscopy & next-generation energy conversion"
      text: |
        Understanding how charge and energy flow through molecular systems,
        nanomaterials, and bulk and interfacial systems is essential for
        designing next-generation energy conversion technologies — from solar
        cells to tunable catalytic transformations.

        We develop theoretical frameworks to expedite, simulate, and decode
        linear and nonlinear spectroscopies and microscopies, directly
        connecting theory to cutting-edge experiments. Our work on ultrafast
        carrier dynamics, trion formation in transition-metal dichalcogenides,
        and symmetry breaking in molecular systems enables the rational
        manipulation of charge and energy flow — with direct applications in
        solar energy conversion and electrochemical catalysis.

  - block: markdown
    content:
      title: "Quantum Sensing for Next-Generation Imaging"
      subtitle: "Precision quantum metrology & open quantum systems"
      text: |
        Near-term quantum technologies are fundamentally limited by
        decoherence — the loss of quantum coherence due to interactions
        with the environment. Precisely measuring and understanding this
        decoherence is both a scientific challenge and a technological
        imperative.

        We develop theoretical tools for precision quantum metrology, enabling
        measurement of temperature, magnetic fields, and electric field
        fluctuations in microscopic environments. These methods reveal
        decoherence mechanisms in quantum devices, probe conformational
        changes near molecular magnets, and detect chemical changes in
        biological and electrochemical systems — connecting quantum sensing
        to chemistry and biology in fundamentally new ways.

        ##### Key Publications

        * [Fast, accurate, and error-resilient variational quantum noise spectroscopy](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0312403)
        * [Numerically exact quantum dynamics with tensor networks: Predicting the decoherence of interacting spin systems](https://pubs.aip.org/aip/jcp/article/164/9/091103/3382073/)

  - block: markdown
    content:
      title: "Mori-Zwanzig Theory"
      subtitle: "Bridging scales with controllable precision"
      text: |
        The Mori-Zwanzig formalism provides a rigorous framework for
        constructing reduced descriptions of complex many-body systems —
        projecting high-dimensional dynamics onto the variables that matter
        most, with explicit control over the approximations made.

        We develop both bottom-up and data-driven approaches within this
        framework to predict the dynamics of complex systems across scales.
        Applications span an unusually broad range: improving semiclassical
        theory, capturing slow biophysical transformations, disentangling
        mechanisms of quantum transport, predicting weather and climate
        dynamics, quantifying and harnessing memory in spectroscopic
        experiments, and building next-generation microscopies that exploit
        multidimensional spectroscopies.

        ##### Key Publications

        * [Data-driven, non-Markovian modelling of weather in the presence of non-stationary, non-Gaussian, and heteroskedastic climate dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.00259)

        * [Efficient formulation of multitime generalized quantum master equations: Taming the cost of simulating 2D spectra](https://doi.org/10.1063/5.0185578)

  - block: markdown
    content:
      title: Our Approach
      subtitle:
      text: |
        Across all four themes, we pursue a unified strategy: constructing
        **physically transparent models** that are both computationally
        efficient and deeply interpretable. Our toolkit spans:

        - **Dimensionality reduction & Mori-Zwanzig theory** — extracting
          essential physics from high-dimensional systems at low costs
        - **Statistical field theories** — treating many-body
          quantum effects and large fluctuating environments rigorously
        - **Molecular dynamics & enhanced sampling** — achieving the
          statistical sampling necessary in the condensed phase
        - **Electronic structure theory** — providing ab initio-level
          chemical accuracy and chemical interpretability
        - **Machine learning** — accelerating simulation by orders of magnitude and cracking inverse problems

        The result is a research program that lowers the cost of challenging
        quantum and condensed-phase simulations by multiple orders of
        magnitude, renders previously inaccessible calculations tractable,
        and delivers the physically intuitive understanding needed to
        manipulate the optical, electronic, and quantum properties of
        complex materials.
